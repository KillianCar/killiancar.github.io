I'm done using your user interface. AI will use your product on my behalf, and if your business has an agent-friendly interface, then you will be at an enormous advantage over the next decade.

I recently rented a car for a trip abroad. My AI agent found eight car rental companies and was able to automatically generate quotes for five of them. Three blocked the non-human traffic, and since I was being forced to go through the now Herculean effort of fumbling around their websites, I just didn't bother.

This trend has been playing out in the professional world in the last couple of years. Companies that have been building their products with agents in mind as first-class users are eating market share from incumbents. You can see this with the enormous success of Attio and Linear, which took an early and aggressive stance towards AI-native SaaS.

Now consumers are using personal agents to drive more of their purchasing decisions. ChatGPT and Claude are morphing into personal assistants and a growing list of Jarvis-style AI agents such as Muse (Meta), Dot (OpenAI), Duet (Anthropic), Grokbot (X), Gemini (Google), and Instinct are vying to be your new entry point for much of the internet. Many of these same companies have trodden this path before, having already fought hard for you to use their web browsers and search engines that have built them the foundations for multitrillion-dollar empires.

There are still times when a user interface makes sense. For example, when an agent has prepared a sensitive operation like making a purchase a human may need to be brought to the user interface for a final confirmation. Information-dense visualisations and dashboards are also unlikely to be replaced by these interfaces, although the major AI labs are pushing to render increasingly complicated visualisations in the previously text-only chat sessions.

Businesses will need to find a delicate balance between allowing legitimate bot traffic and preventing malicious actors. Implementing emergent standards in your product, such as Universal Commerce Protocol and Personal Agent Protocol, will become table stakes to succeed. Many have seen this coming and have embedded their own home-grown chat assistants in their websites; however, these are often frustrating user experiences. These limited chatbots are missing the important personal context that helps them operate on their users’ behalf. Customers won't want to explain to every chatbot what their travel plans are, what allergies they have, or retype their home address. Their own agents have access to this information already and they will find the path of least resistance to purchasing a product, whether from your business or a competitor's.

Walmart just launched a way to shop using your own personal assistants rather than through their own website, and Google's recent earnings report in which they announced a slowdown in revenue growth from search advertising signals that the tide is already turning.

Agents are here. They're banging on your door trying to give you money. Let them in.
